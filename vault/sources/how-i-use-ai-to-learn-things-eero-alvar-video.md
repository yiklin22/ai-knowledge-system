# How I Use AI to Learn Things (Eero Alvar video)

## Takeaways
- Argument: teaching from one outlet to many learners cannot fit any one learner, and learning from many outlets costs switching effort and trust, so the ideal is one adaptive teacher per learner delivering all sources through one interface.
- Design principles: optimise the teaching (work at the edge of the learner's understanding) and optimise allocation of mental effort (keep struggle in the material, not in logistics, planning or fact-checking).
- Three phases: probe (graded multiple-choice quiz that narrows down what the learner knows), plan (reason out the path and show it as a Mermaid graph, partly to force the AI to reason it out), teach (one reasoning step at a time, with periodic quizzes).
- Verification and fact-checking run in sub-agents; diagram sub-agents look at the SVG they drew to check it.
- Demo topic: differential forms, in the Pi harness with Obsidian logging; the presenter says model intelligence matters for teaching. Anecdotal: one learner, one topic, no learning outcome measured.
- The trust claim (identical explanation learned better from a familiar source) is an illustration by the presenter, not tested.
